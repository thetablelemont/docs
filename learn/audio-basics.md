Four ideas cover most of what you need to run sound well: cables, gain, EQ and compression. Learn them in that order.

## Cables: over-under

Wrap cables over-under: alternate the direction of each loop. The cable lies flat, comes out straight next time, and lasts years longer. It's the first thing anyone in AV notices about you.

## Gain staging

The most important thing in audio. Set each input's level at the source, before any other processing.

Signal flows **source → gain → processing → fader → output**. Every output (house, monitors, stream) starts from the same gain, so get it right once.

- Aim for **green to yellow** on the meter.
- Too low gets you a weak, noisy signal.
- Too hot gets you distortion that nothing downstream can fix.

## EQ

EQ shapes tone, not volume.

- **Lows (20–250 Hz):** weight, boom. Kick, bass, room rumble.
- **Mids (250 Hz–4 kHz):** most of the sound. Clarity or mud happens here.
- **Highs (4–20 kHz):** air and detail. Cymbals, consonants.

**Cut before you boost.** If something sounds muddy, cut the low-mids instead of boosting the highs.

## Compression

Compression evens out loud and quiet moments so you're not riding the fader.

- **Threshold:** where it starts working. Lower means more compression.
- **Ratio:** how hard it turns down. 2:1 is gentle, 4:1 is moderate, 10:1 and up is limiting.

Use it on vocals, speech and drums.
